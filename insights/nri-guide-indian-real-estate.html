NRI Guide to Indian Real Estate: FEMA Regulations, Repatriation & Capital Gains

A practical legal and financial roadmap for Non-Resident Indians investing in Noida, Greater Noida & Delhi NCR: RBI approvals, permitted banking routes, Section 195 TDS compliance, repatriation procedures, and consular Power of Attorney.

For Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) and Overseas Citizens of India (OCIs) based in North America, the Middle East, the UK, and Southeast Asia, investing in Indian real estate is no longer merely an emotional retirement gesture. It has evolved into a strategic hedge against currency depreciation and a vehicle for portfolio diversification, bolstered by institutional regulatory frameworks like the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act (RERA).

However, navigating cross-border real estate transactions requires strict adherence to statutory Indian laws—specifically the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA), the Income Tax Act, 1961, and state stamp duty and registration guidelines. A misstep in banking routes or tax withholding can lead to frozen bank balances, delayed registry, or penalties from enforcement directorates.

Below, the Urban Investors NRI Wealth Desk provides an objective breakdown of the legal mechanics governing property acquisition, taxation, and funds repatriation in Delhi NCR.

Legal & Tax Disclaimer

Tax laws, bilateral Double Taxation Avoidance Agreements (DTAA), and RBI foreign exchange directives are subject to legislative amendment. This guide reflects verified regulations as of October 2026. Individual investors must consult a qualified Chartered Accountant (CA) or FEMA attorney regarding their specific jurisdictional residency status.

1. Who Can Buy Property Under FEMA: Permitted & Prohibited Assets

Under general permission granted by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) through FEMA regulations:

  • Eligible Entities: Any Non-Resident Indian (an Indian citizen residing outside India) and any Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) can freely purchase any number of residential properties (apartments, villas, floors) and commercial properties (office suites, retail shops, warehouses) in India.
  • No Prior Approval Required: You do not need to seek permission from the RBI or file pre-acquisition declarations.
  • Strict Prohibitions: NRIs and OCIs are strictly prohibited from purchasing agricultural land, plantation property, or farm houses in India. Such properties may only be acquired through lawful inheritance from a resident Indian.

2. Permitted Banking Routes: NRE, NRO & Inward Remittances

Under FEMA guidelines, property transactions must be funded entirely through legitimate banking channels. Cash payments, foreign currency handovers, or hawala channels are illegal and void property contracts.

Understanding NRE vs. NRO Accounts

NRIs should understand how their account types dictate ease of future fund movement:

  • NRE (Non-Resident External) Account: Maintained in Indian Rupees, funded entirely through foreign currency remittances. Funds deposited in NRE accounts are freely repatriable outside India at any time without ceiling. If you purchase an apartment using NRE funds, the original invested capital can be remitted back abroad smoothly upon sale.
  • NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) Account: Designed for managing income earned in India (such as rental income, dividends, or domestic sale proceeds). Funds are held in Rupees. While rental income in an NRO account can be repatriated after paying taxes, capital proceeds are subject to the standard RBI limit.
  • FCNR (Foreign Currency Non-Resident) Deposits: Term deposits maintained in foreign currencies (USD, GBP, EUR) that can also be liquidated to fund verified property acquisitions.

3. Taxation & Section 195 TDS Compliance

Taxation is the area that causes the greatest confusion for overseas buyers, particularly when selling an Indian property or purchasing from another non-resident.

TDS on Purchasing from an NRI Seller (Section 195)

When a buyer purchases property from a resident Indian, standard TDS is 1% under Section 194-IA. However, if the seller is an NRI, Section 195 applies:

  • The buyer is legally required to deduct TDS at the full capital gains rate—typically 20% (plus applicable surcharge and 4% health & education cess) for long-term capital assets, or 30% plus surcharge for short-term assets.
  • If the seller does not compute actual gains, withholding tax may be demanded on the entire sale consideration.
  • Lower Deduction Certificate (Section 197): To avoid excessive tax withholding, the NRI seller should apply online for a Lower/Nil Deduction Certificate via Form 13 with the Income Tax Department well before the registry date.

Capital Gains Tax Rates

Real estate held for more than 24 months is classified as a Long-Term Capital Asset. Under the updated tax framework:

  • Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG): Taxed at a flat rate of 12.5% without indexation benefits for transfers.
  • Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG): Real estate held for 24 months or less is taxed at the individual's applicable slab rates.
  • Tax Exemption Routes: Capital gains tax can be legally deferred or exempted by reinvesting net gains in another residential property in India under Section 54 (subject to statutory limits), or by investing in government capital gains bonds under Section 54EC (up to ₹50 Lacs within 6 months of sale).

4. Repatriation of Sale Proceeds Outside India

One of the most common concerns for NRIs is: "Can I take my money back to the US, UK, or Dubai when I sell my apartment?"

The answer is an unequivocal yes, provided the transaction complies with FEMA repatriation regulations:

  1. Acquisition Funded via NRE / Inward Remittance: The foreign exchange equivalent of the original acquisition price can be repatriated freely outside India through the Authorized Dealer (AD) bank. Any capital appreciation above the principal can be repatriated through the NRO route.
  2. The USD $1,000,000 Annual NRO Scheme: Under RBI regulations, an NRI can repatriate up to USD $1 Million (or equivalent) per financial year from their NRO account balances.
  3. Mandatory Documentation (Form 15CA & 15CB): Before the bank executes the outward foreign remittance, a qualified Indian Chartered Accountant must verify tax compliance and issue Form 15CB, and the remitter must file the electronic declaration on Form 15CA with the Income Tax portal.

5. Remote Transactions & Power of Attorney (PoA) Execution

Over 85% of NRI property transactions in Noida and Greater Noida are executed without the buyer physically traveling to India. This is made possible through a legally valid Special Power of Attorney (PoA).

How to Execute an Enforceable PoA from Abroad

  1. Drafting the Specific PoA: Draft a Specific (not General) Power of Attorney detailing the exact property parcel, builder name, unit number, and permissible actions (execution of agreement to sell, builder correspondence, registration before the Sub-Registrar).
  2. Consular Attestation or Apostille: Sign the document before an authorized consular officer at the nearest Indian Embassy or Consulate. Alternatively, in countries that are signatories to the Hague Apostille Convention, the PoA can be notarized and apostilled by the local foreign ministry.
  3. Adjudication in India: Once physically couriered to India, the nominated attorney must present the document to the District Sub-Registrar within 3 months of receipt for adjudication and payment of applicable local stamp duty.
